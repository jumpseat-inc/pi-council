## Intent

Thresholds move only after the evidence that motivated the move is on the
record. A pre-registration record names the policy version it introduces, the
coefficient or floor that changed, the ledger evidence that motivated the
change, and what outcome the change is expected to produce; the policy file's
version must match a record or validation refuses to pass. The point is to make
tuning falsifiable — a version bumped with no record is a threshold moved by
taste, and the refusal is what keeps the ledger the basis for changes rather
than decoration beside them.

The human-visible surface is the validation line: it is a maintainer's only
signal at the moment they are stopped, so it names the version, the file, and
the remedy rather than gesturing at "policy drift". A line that names only the
category sends the maintainer to open another file to learn what to do, which is
the same failure the repository's existing drift line was written to avoid.

## Acceptance

A policy version with a matching registration entry validates clean; the same
file with the version bumped and no entry fails with the named line; a
registration entry naming a coefficient and a ledger citation round-trips; the
check runs inside the existing `python3 council/validate.py` invocation; and no
threshold in the packaged default policy lacks a registration entry.

## Intent

/council bounds its deliberation — a fixed round cap, a convergence state,
and a deterministic stop so a session cannot exchange forever. Once EV-10
puts seats into the decomposition, that same bound is required here — the
maintainer asked for the session to be bounded similar to the /council
command, and an unbounded multi-seat exchange would make /features-new
cost- and time-unpredictable at exactly the moment it feeds
/features-deliver's unattended runs.

This card adds the bounds to the rewritten procedure text — a stated round
cap, a convergence criterion (what the facilitator treats as settled enough
to draft), and a defined fallback when the cap is hit without convergence
(the facilitator drafts anyway and carries the open disagreements into the
step-3 draft pass for the human to settle).

Open questions for the council — the cap value (council.md's own cap is the
natural precedent), whether the fallback draft is written by the
facilitator alone or by the most-advanced seat position, and whether
non-convergence should be surfaced to the human as a distinct warning.

User-visible surface — none beyond the draft pass; the bound shows up as
run behavior and, when hit, as a note in the presented draft. Depends on
EV-10.

## Acceptance

- The procedure text states a numeric round cap, a convergence criterion,
  and a fallback outcome for non-convergence.
- The stated bound matches the deliberation bounds of council.md in shape
  (fixed cap, explicit stop) rather than inventing a new scheme.
- python3 council/validate.py stays green; step 3's gate text unchanged.

## Phase 1 rulings (recorded 2026 by the human before the run, immutable for this run, binding on every seat including steward)

- **Ruling CAP-1 (round cap):** the decomposition session is bounded at
  **3 rounds, the same exchange cap /council uses** — one consistent bound
  across the council's loops, per the council.md precedent named in this
  card's Intent.
- **Ruling FALLBACK-1 (non-convergence):** when the cap is hit without
  convergence, the **facilitator drafts the decomposition anyway** and
  carries every open disagreement into the step-3 draft pass, **labeled as
  unresolved**, for the human to settle at the approval gate the command
  already has. No new gate is created.

> ## Reframe
>
> The proposed design needs one concrete correction: **do not write the bound as a cap on exchange rounds, and do not import council.md's "stop early if stabilized."**
>
> 1. **The cap counts waves, not exchanges.** The three waves *are* the three rounds. The bound text reads: "exactly three waves; no wave is re-run; no fourth dispatch." > 2. **Convergence is a label at the fixed endpoint, not a stopping condition.** council.md's "stop early if stabilised" has **no valid analog** here, because wave 3 is unconditional — SEATS-1 binds "all four seats dispatched every run," and the card's own step 2 says product-owner rules "last, unconditionally." >
> 3. **Convergence = zero open disagreements — not unanimity.** "Open" = not ruled by product-owner (ratify/amend, dissent named) AND not settled by a runnable check. A named dissent that *received a ruling* is converged. This matters because product-owner's whole format is "dissent named"; if "any dissent" meant non-converged, then every run where skeptic objects forces the fallback, and the "convergence criterion" collapses into a tautology. > 4. **The fallback author question is already answered by the wave structure — and FALLBACK-1's wording needs one reconciliation.** "Facilitator drafts anyway" must be read as *the facilitator completes the same mechanical verbatim aggregation and proceeds to the step-3 pass* — **not** "facilitator authors card text," which would collide with the hard "facilitator authors nothing" mandate and the attribution-free Part 1 pin. > **Approach**
>

So EV-11 must **not** introduce an exchange loop with a cap on it — it must declare the three waves *to be* the three rounds, and define convergence/fallback as properties of that fixed pipeline. Concretely, two insertions into step 2 of `features-new.md`, both inside the EV-10 seam (between dispatch and aggregation), neither touching step 3:
>
> 1. **One sentence in the step-2 intro** (line ~40, after "in three waves. You route, wait, aggregate verbatim, and author nothing."): the session is bounded at three waves = three rounds, the same cap `/council`'s step 3 uses; disagreements are ruled in wave 3, never exchanged — no seat is re-dispatched to respond to another seat's position. This sentence is load-bearing and must precede wave 2, because the facilitator's strongest pattern-match temptation is council.md's step-3 re-dispatch loop, and it arises *between* wave 2 and wave 3 — a block placed after wave 3 would be read too late.
>
> 2. **A "Bounded session" block between Wave 3 (line 90) and Aggregation (line 106)** — the seam's literal insertion point — carrying the convergence criterion, the fallback, and the cap-hit note.
>
> **How the cap counts.** The round is the wave, never the dispatch and never an exchange iteration. Wave 1 = round 1, wave 2 = round 2 (its two parallel dispatches are *one* round — the text must say this, or a dispatch-counting facilitator miscounts at 4), wave 3 = round 3. A stalled dispatch's cancel + one re-dispatch (dispatch discipline, line 142) is not a round. The cap is therefore never "approached" — it is the structure; the fallback is about reaching the end of the structure without convergence, not about exceeding it. Deliberately *not* imported from council.md: its "stop early if positions have stabilised" — features-new has no early stop, because wave 3 "rules, last, unconditionally" (line 90). The shape match with council.md is fixed cap + explicit deterministic stop (the session always ends after wave 3, converged or not), not early-stop.
>
> **Convergence criterion.** The session has converged when wave 3 has ruled every open-judgment dispute the attackers surfaced — epic goal and each child's `state` ratified or amended, each dispute ruled with dissent named — so that the aggregation's disagreement ledger contains only **named dissents** (seats that did not endorse), never unruled disputes. This is mechanically checkable by the facilitator: any dispute in the ledger without a wave-3 ruling = non-converged. Non-convergence's concrete instance is product-owner escalation (its `<escalation>` forbids portfolio change, reversing a recorded human decision, or a defective goal; per SEATS-1 that escalation goes to the human). FALLBACK-1 then subsumes escalation: the facilitator drafts anyway, and the escalated item rides to the step-3 gate as an unresolved disagreement — no separate Needs-Human stop, because unlike council.md (whose human gate is step 11), features-new's step-3 approval gate *is* the human decision point. "No new gate is created" is exactly this reasoning, and it is consistent, not a weakening.
>
> **Fallback authorship.** The fallback draft is the **mechanical aggregation of all recorded contributions** — identical to the converged case, including wave 3's amendments — never a facilitator-authored synthesis and never a "most-advanced seat position" base. Picking product-owner's amended draft as the base would be the facilitator choosing a winner, which the facilitator is forbidden to do (council.md: "The facilitator decides nothing"; EV-10: "authors nothing at any step"). The wave-3-amended draft naturally dominates the aggregation anyway — but as a property of the aggregation, not a facilitator choice. The fallback changes only the ledger content, never the wave structure and never Part 1: the unresolved items reuse the existing `Decision: unresolved — your call` markers (line 114's ledger contract), and the cap-hit note is a Part 2 line, never Part 1 card text (the attribution-free mandate at line 127 is untouched).
>
> **Distinct warning — yes, one line.** The existing `Decision: unresolved — your call` markers cannot distinguish "converged with named dissents" from "cap hit without convergence", and the human's decision posture differs between the two (approving a settled draft vs. settling disputes the seats could not). The card's own user-visible surface says "when hit, as a note in the presented draft". So: one line at the top of the ledger surface — the session did not converge within its round cap; the listed disagreements are unresolved and require the human's decision. Presentation-only, never written to disk, never a new gate.
>
> **Tradeoffs accepted**
>
> - **No exchange loop at all** — a re-dispatch exchange could in principle sharpen the decomposition, but it is what the card's goal forbids (cost/time unpredictability feeding `/features-deliver`'s unattended runs) and what the EV-10 seam explicitly excludes. The bound is worth more than the marginal convergence quality.
> - **Escalation rides to the gate instead of stopping the run** — a facilitator might otherwise halt on product-owner escalation; FALLBACK-1's draft-anyway means the human sees a full draft plus the unresolved marker at the one gate they already own. The cost is that a defective-goal escalation produces a draft of a defective goal — but the human authors the goal's source (`$ARGUMENTS`) and can edit/reject at the gate, so the risk is contained.
> - **No early-stop** — the session always pays for wave 3 even when wave 2 surfaces nothing. That is the price of "unconditionally" and of a deterministic stop; it is one dispatch, and it buys the ruling that makes the ledger complete.

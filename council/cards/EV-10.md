## Intent

Today steps 1 and 2 of council/procedures/features-new.md are executed by
the facilitator solo — one agent reads the template and board, slices the
children, drafts every goal, and presents the result. This card rewrites
those steps so the decomposition is deliberated — dispatched seats
(council_dispatch + council_wait, already available to the parent session,
no engine change) contribute to the slicing, the goal sentences, the state
assignments, and the designer-surface flags in each child's Intent, and the
facilitator aggregates their input into the single draft pass step 3
presents.

"Interested agents" is deliberately left as the deliberation's first open
question — the seats should rule on which seats participate (all nine, a
default subset, or a content-driven selection rule) and how a seat's
disagreement is carried into the draft pass rather than silently resolved
by the facilitator.

User-visible surface — the human's draft pass itself; the set of cards
presented at step 3 now carries seat attributions and named disagreements.
No TUI, no copy strings; whether this warrants a designer seat is the
council's call, flagged here explicitly.

The draft-then-confirm gate is not part of this card's surface — step 3's
text (write nothing to disk until the human approves) must survive this
rewrite verbatim.

**Approach**

The rewrite keeps the mechanics and moves all *content authorship* into seats. Step 1 keeps its mechanics (read `_template.md`, read `board.md`, assign `EPIC-<n>`) but stops drafting the epic goal solo — the epic goal is a seat contribution like everything else. The rewritten step 2 becomes: **(a)** dispatch `product-owner`, `designer`, `principal`, `skeptic` in parallel, one round, identical input — the feature (`$ARGUMENTS`), the template, the board, the assigned epic id, the procedural bars (goal falsifiability, no `: `, state rules, Intent-surface rule, em-dash/board rules), and a **mandated output shape** (children list with per-child scope/goal/state/surface flag + the epic goal) — with *no facilitator draft and no other seat's position* in any input (council.md step 2's independence precedent, `council/procedures/council.md:47-52`); **(b)** `council_wait` all four job ids, jobs bounded per the house dispatch discipline with each job id noted (`council.md:24-27`); **(c)** aggregate by **mechanical concordance** — children aligned by stated scope, agreeing elements drafted from the agreement and attributed to the seats whose text produced them, single-source elements attributed to their proposer, and every *conflicting* position on the same element (exists-vs-not, different goal, different state, different surface flag) recorded as a **named disagreement** with both sides and their job ids — never resolved by paraphrase, because *the facilitator paraphrasing a seat's line is silent resolution wearing punctuation*; **(d)** route named disagreements: what `product-owner` may rule is ruled via a bounded **ruling dispatch** (dissent + options-rejected recorded, per `product-owner.md`'s own format), what its escalation criteria forbid (portfolio change, wrong goal, reversing a recorded human decision) escalates to the human per SEATS-1 ("reached through the orchestrator" = the facilitator of this command stops and surfaces, the `council.md` Needs-Human shape); **(e)** assemble the step-3 draft: cards exactly as-written-to-disk + per-card attribution lines + a named-disagreement ledger (dispute, both sides named, ruling-or-human-open status). The attribution mandate lives in the rewritten step 2's aggregation language — **step 3's text is byte-identical**, and attribution is *presentation at the gate, never card-file content* (writing "contributed by X" into an Intent would corrupt exactly the `Intent` surface-reading `/features-deliver` depends on, `features-new.md:29-35`).

**Decisions on the four open questions**

1. **Dispatch structure / job ids on record.** Text inlines the house bounded-dispatch discipline (council.md precedent of restating, not cross-referencing — `council-runner.md:216-219` does the same): `council_dispatch` → note the returned job id → `council_wait` with a window → cancel + one re-dispatch on stall → stop and surface on double-fail. Job ids are recoverable artifacts regardless — each dispatch writes a `RunManifest` (`runs/<runId>/<jobId>.json`, seat field) under the run substrate (`vault/wiki/run-transcripts.md`), and the existing smoke harness already greps manifests as dispatch evidence, so SMOKE-1's "job ids captured from that scratch run" needs no new mechanism. The draft names *seats* (the human-visible surface); job ids ride in the facilitator's record and the manifests.
2. **Contributions → draft pass.** Every element in the presented draft names its contributing seats; the input's mandated output shape is what makes the contributions *comparable* — without a fixed shape, aggregation is the facilitator inventing structure, which is the defect this card removes.
3. **Disagreements.** Two-tier: `product-owner` rules what it may rule (a ruling dispatch, explicitly *not* an exchange round — the EV-11 seam), dissent always named; what it may not rule escalates to the human per SEATS-1 and is presented as an open item at the gate, where the human's edit/approve power settles it. No facilitator middle.
4. **What the facilitator aggregates.** Agreeing text (verbatim, attributed) + mechanical assembly (ids, card list, state mechanics) + a concordance diff that *produces* the disagreements. The facilitator writes no goal, state, or surface sentence of its own.

**Seam for EV-11 and step-3 integrity.** The first pass is named "round 1"; aggregation consumes "all recorded contributions;" the disagreement path is a ruling dispatch, not an exchange round — so EV-11's bounded-exchange text inserts between dispatch and aggregation without touching this card's written surfaces, and this card writes no cap/convergence/fallback language (EPIC-3 scope guard). `validate.py` parses cards and board only, never procedures, so the step-3 byte-identity is the real gate and must be pinned by test, not by eyeball.

**Tradeoffs accepted**

- **Cost**: four full independent decompositions + an occasional ruling dispatch is an order of magnitude more expensive than the solo decomposition it replaces, and independent granularity will diverge on fuzzy features — the step-3 gate becomes partial triage. Worth it: solo-facilitator resolution is the exact defect the card removes, and named disagreement is the product.
- **Attribution not persisted in cards**: who contributed what survives only in run transcripts (pruned to 15 runs) and the gate presentation, so post-hoc audit of a decomposition needs the transcript. Accepted deliberately — card files are the loop's durable state and must stay clean for `/features-deliver` and `validate.py`.
- **Verbatim, not summarized**: I accept that the draft may be longer and rougher (four seats' raw sentences) than a polished solo draft. Polish is the facilitator's favorite silent-resolution vector.

## Principal seat — EV-10 position

### Cross-seam reading

The seam here is prose-to-prose, not prose-to-API. The engine half of this contract is thin and does not change:

- `council_dispatch` returns a job id + seat name (`Dispatched ${seat.name} as ${job.id}`; `details: { jobId, seat }`) and `council_wait` returns a `formatReport` whose payload is free text under `--- output ---` (`extensions/hub-tools.ts`). There is **no structured field** distinguishing "contribution" from "disagreement" from "ruling." The only thing that ever carries that distinction is prose the facilitator writes.
- None of the four seats SEATS-1 names holds a hub grant: `product-owner`/`designer` have `Read, Grep, Glob, Write`, `principal` has `Read, Grep, Glob`, `skeptic` has `Read, Grep, Glob, Bash` — no `task`/`hub`, no `spawns` (`council/agents/*.md`; `grantsFor` in `extensions/seats.ts`). So the facilitator is the *sole* dispatcher, exactly as `council.md`'s "the facilitator routes" and `[[facilitator]]` state. That is fine and should stay.
- The current `features-new.md` steps 1–2 contain **zero dispatch** — the facilitator alone reads template/board, assigns the `EPIC` id, writes the epic goal, and decomposes children. Step 3's gate block ("Write nothing to disk until the human approves…") is the text that must survive byte-identical, so **every new requirement must be a property of the draft's content specified in steps 1–2, never an edit to step 3.**
- The output contract that flows downstream is load-bearing: a child's `state` is consumed by `council.md` step 1 (`state == Ready` hard-gates a Council run) and by `features-deliver.md`'s unattended loop, and the surface flag lives in `Intent` prose consumed by `council.md` step 1's surface-touching test. So `state` and the `Intent` surface naming are **seam-decisions made at decomposition and consumed later with no re-check** — they are not local bookkeeping. `validate.py` enforces shape (`REQUIRED_KEYS` = id/title/state/owner/epic/goal, `ID_RE`, `STATE_COLUMNS`, no `: ` in goal, board em-dash) but validates **cards/board only** — it cannot see a malformed `features-new.md`.

### Blind spots

- **Procedure-text author (human-facing contract slice).** From inside the markdown, "dispatch seats to contribute" reads as a single call whose result arrives shaped. It cannot see that `council_wait` returns free text and that three of the four seat bodies are written for *existing-card* deliberation — `product-owner` "does not design the solution," `skeptic` needs a claim to attack, `designer` needs an artifact to critique (`council/agents/*.md`). Naive parallel-generation would hand those seats a task their bodies disclaim and force the facilitator to *reconcile* four incompatible drafts — which is resolution, not aggregation.
- **Engine slice (hub-tools.ts / seats.ts).** Sees dispatch/wait/cancel as generic spawn-collect; cannot see that the semantic contract (attribution, disagreement-preservation) is entirely in procedure prose it never reads. It will faithfully return seat text and have no notion whether the facilitator merged it or silently dropped it.
- **Facilitator slice (the parent agent executing the procedure).** It is the single writer and the same model-class that would have decomposed alone. Nothing in the engine stops it from narrating "I dispatched the seats" without calling `council_dispatch`, or from paraphrasing-and-merging seat output instead of preserving it. Only the procedure text constrains it, so only the smoke harness's *job-records-on-disk* check can catch a narrated-but-not-executed dispatch.

### Reframe

The card's own wording — "the facilitator aggregates their input into the single draft pass step 3 presents" — already forbids the naive reading of four parallel decomposition generators. Make that explicit: **generate-then-attack, not parallel-generate.**

1. Facilitator produces a **first-pass decomposition** (epic + candidate children with goals, states, surface flags) — preserving the exact output shape steps 1–2 already define, so step 3's gate is untouched.
2. Dispatch `principal`, `designer`, `skeptic` **in parallel against that same first pass**, each in its own charter: principal reframes the seam cuts (does any child's `goal` bake in another slice's assumption; is the epic `goal` the whole feature and not one child's slice), designer flags which children are surface-touching and argues the `Intent` must name the screen/copy/state (the existing step-2 bullet, now seated), skeptic attacks each `goal` for falsifiability/stub/colon-space and each `state` against the Ready-vs-Backlog bar with **runnable checks against the draft text itself** (colon-space grep, `REQUIRED_KEYS` presence, `ID_RE`).
3. Dispatch `product-owner` **last**, with the aggregated disputes only — it rules, it doesn't generate (its charter: "you do not design the solution"; `when_invoked` #4 already covers `Backlog → Ready` ratification).
4. Facilitator aggregates **verbatim, labeled by seat** — reuse `council.md` step 2's exact convention ("append each returned position… verbatim, labeled by seat") — and carries anything `product-owner` does not rule as a **named, attributed open item into the step-3 draft**. That is precisely the SEATS-1 escalation path: the facilitator *is* the orchestrator proxy in `/features-new`, so "escalation to the CEO (the human), reached through the orchestrator" = the open item reaches the human at the draft gate, where the human's edit/drop/approve authority already lives.

This structure respects SEATS-1 (same four seats, named explicitly), matches the seat charters rather than fighting them, and is round-minimal by construction — a single first-pass → seat-input → ruling → draft pass — so the sibling card can insert an explicit round bound without rewriting the attribution contract.

4. **Attribution reaches the draft.** After the smoke run, the presented draft must contain, per disputed child/field, a seat label and verbatim-or-quoted seat text; a draft where seat input is paraphrased into the facilitator's voice fails.
5. **Disagreement is named, not resolved.** Where `principal` and `designer` disagree on whether a child is surface-touching (or `skeptic` blocks a `goal`), and `product-owner` does not rule it, the draft must contain an explicit "unresolved" item naming both seats; absence of any such item when the seat transcripts show a disagreement fails.

#### Round 1 — designer (job-1.3)

VERBATIM:

The person path through the step-3 draft pass:

1. **Arrival.** Human just typed `/features-new` with a feature description. They expect a list of cards they need to approve, edit, or reject — not a transcript of who deliberated.
2. **First glance.** They scan the set: epic at top, children grouped under it. They want to immediately see scope (how many cards), the goal of each, and any red flags.
3. **First action.** They read the epic's goal sentence. Move to the first child. Compare goals against the original feature description.
4. **Feedback moment.** They hit a child where the goal is contested. They want to know: contested by whom, on what dimension (scope? testability? surface?), and what their options are.
5. **Approval moment.** They need to know what "approve" means on a contested card — does approval acknowledge the disagreement, ignore it, or override it?

The Gulf of Execution is wide today: the human cannot tell what approving a card commits them to, because the card doesn't reveal its deliberation history. The Gulf of Evaluation is also wide: there's no signal that an item is contested, so the human approves the set without noticing.

**Design position**

Each card in the step-3 draft pass must carry three things, surfaced as a small structured block at the top of the card's `Intent` section so they appear before the goal sentence is read: **(1) a `Contributors` line naming every seat whose dispatch produced a substantive contribution to the card** (the seats named by ruling SEATS-1: product-owner, designer, principal, skeptic — a seat that did not contribute is omitted, not listed as silent), **(2) a `Disagreements` block listing any seat that did not endorse the card as drafted**, where each disagreement is a one- or two-line note naming the seat and the dimension (scope, testability, surface, state-assignment, etc.) — never a long paragraph, never silently folded into the goal, and **(3) an explicit `Decision: unresolved — your call` marker on every line of the disagreement block** so the human can see at a glance that the facilitator carried the disagreement forward rather than resolving it. The facilitator aggregates by **placing** these blocks, never by **adjudicating** them — the procedure text must forbid the facilitator from rewriting, paraphrasing, or omitting a seat's stated disagreement, and must require that the seat's own wording (or a faithful one-line restatement) is what appears in the block. Per-seat labels appear only inside these two blocks, never on goal sentences or state assignments, so a card reads as one unit and the trace is opt-in to read.

**Gulf closed**

Evaluation, for the human at the moment of approving the draft pass. Specifically: closing the gulf between *"the facilitator showed me a set of cards"* and *"I can tell which of these cards had a seat that did not agree with the draft, and what they disagreed about"*. A secondary execution gulf closes for the same person at the same moment: between *"I approve this set"* and *"I know whether my approval resolves, ignores, or is asked to rule on each disagreement"* — the `Decision: unresolved — your call` marker makes the act of approving a contested card an explicit acknowledgement that the disagreement is being handed up rather than smoothed over.

**Principle and evidence**

- **Signifier + feedback (the principle).** A `Contributors` line and a `Disagreements` block are visible signifiers of deliberation state. They turn the cards from opaque drafts into a document where the human can read the trace before deciding.
- **Knowledge in the world (beats knowledge in the head) (the principle).** "A seat disagreed" must not be a thing the human has to infer by reading carefully — it is a thing the surface must say.
- **Constraint by procedure text (the principle).** The smallest fix for "the facilitator silently resolves disagreement" is a procedure-level rule that forbids the facilitator from paraphrasing or omitting a seat's disagreement. The card carries what the seat said; the facilitator cannot blur it.
- **Slip vs. mistake (the principle).** Today's failure is a mistake class: the human approves a set believing all seats agreed because the surface does not say otherwise. The fix is making the conceptual model visible at the moment of decision.
- **Evidence (the artifact).** `council/procedures/features-new.md` step 3 ("Present the full draft of the epic and every child — complete frontmatter and `Intent` section, exactly as each would be written to disk — to the human in one pass") does not require any seat attribution or disagreement surface today; step 2's prose ("a single testable `goal`", "`state` set per `/board-create-card` step 4") defines the per-card shape that the draft pass inherits. The card's `Intent` section is the right insertion point because step 3 names `Intent` explicitly as part of the presented draft and `council.md` step 1 already reads `Intent` to decide whether `designer` is seated — so an `Intent` block that names contributors also strengthens that downstream decision.

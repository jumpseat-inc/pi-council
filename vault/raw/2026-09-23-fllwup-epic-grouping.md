management: curation reduces the count, grouping gives the residue delivery

## What was learned

1. **Backlog management is a two-phase pipeline — curate, then group.** Curation
   merges/retires near-duplicates; grouping assigns the residue to thematic
   delivery epics. Neither is sufficient alone.
2. **Grouping repairs the `epic: null` orphans curation creates.** Curation nulls
   `epic:` on cross-epic merges; grouping re-homes all 41 cards, including those
   8 orphans. Curation trades origin attribution for consolidation; grouping
   restores a *home* — but not the *origin*.
3. **Origin attribution becomes prose-only.** A child's `epic:` now points at its
   new thematic epic; the "filed by EPIC-N" lineage survives only in the new
   epic's `Intent`.
4. **A close-out epic's `goal` is aggregate.** New EPIC-16…22 goals roll up their
   children's outcomes ("the filed residuals are all delivered — X, Y, Z …
   gates green"), unlike a feature epic's single falsifiable sentence. This is a
   distinct epic flavor generated by backlog close-out.
5. **Ids are allocated at fetched HEAD** → EPIC-16…22; all 7 epics are `Backlog`
   and no children are promoted yet.
6. **A subsystem group is a runnable scope.** Grouping by subsystem
   (`council/gate/**`, `extensions/gate*.ts`, the usage store, etc.) makes each
   epic a coherent `/features-deliver` unit rather than a mixed bag.

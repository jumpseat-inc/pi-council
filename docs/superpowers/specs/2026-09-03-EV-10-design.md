# EV-10 — features-new decomposition dispatches the council (design)

Settled design for card EV-10 (epic EPIC-3). This document is the write-up
of the Council's deliberation (rounds 1–3, Skeptic O1–O5, consolidator
synthesis, step-6 product-owner ruling). It derives nothing new; it records
what the deliberation settled and what the step-6 ruling bound. An owner
reading only this file must not be able to reach two different designs.

## Goal (from the card)

> The features-new procedure dispatches council seats to deliberate on the
> epic and child decomposition and the draft presented to the human records
> each contributing seat and any unresolved disagreement instead of the
> facilitator decomposing alone

## Binding rulings (immutable for this run)

- **SEATS-1** — the seats that participate in a /features-new decomposition
  are **product-owner, designer, principal, and skeptic**. If the
  product-owner requires it, escalation goes to the CEO (the human), reached
  through the orchestrator. No other seats deliberate by default; the
  procedure must name this set explicitly.
- **SMOKE-1** — the acceptance smoke run executes /features-new against a
  **scratch copy of council/** (temp directory), never the real board.
  Evidence for the dispatched-job-ids criterion is the job ids captured
  from that scratch run. The real `council/board.md` is not modified by any
  smoke run.
- **Step-6 ruling (wave-1 authorship)** — the first decomposition artifact
  is authored by `principal` alone, dispatched once in wave 1. Its output is
  the child decomposition (slicing, per-child `goal`, `state`, surface flag)
  in principal's native `Reframe` format, plus the epic goal as a one-line
  transcription of the human's intake. Wave 2 dispatches `skeptic` +
  `designer` in parallel against that artifact (identical input, their
  native formats, the completeness charter). Wave 3 dispatches
  `product-owner` last, unconditionally, ruling-only — ratify/amend the epic
  goal and each child's `state`, rule disputes with dissent named, escalate
  per its `<escalation>` to the human per SEATS-1. The facilitator authors
  nothing, at any step. Attribution and the disagreement ledger live at the
  gate presentation and the `runs/` transcript, never in the card file.

## Scope

The rewrite covers `council/procedures/features-new.md` **steps 1 and 2**
only, plus a regression test pinning step 3's byte-identity. Step 3's
draft-then-confirm text is **byte-identical** to today's (583-byte block,
pinned by test). Steps 0, 4, and 5 are unchanged. No engine code changes
(`council_dispatch`/`council_wait` are already available to the parent
session). No seat body changes (skeptic.md's falsifiability charter is
untouched — see the completeness-charter scoping below).

**EV-11 seam (scope guard).** Round-cap/convergence/fallback text is the
surface of sibling card EV-11 (same epic) and is **not drafted here**. The
rewrite must leave room for EV-11's bounded-exchange text to insert between
dispatch and aggregation: the first pass is named **wave 1**; aggregation
consumes "all recorded contributions"; the disagreement path is a **ruling
dispatch, not an exchange round**. This card writes no cap/convergence/
fallback language.

## The rewrite

### Step 1 — Create the epic card (rewritten)

Mechanics unchanged: read `council/cards/_template.md` for the frontmatter
shape and `council/board.md` for current state; assign the next `EPIC-<n>`
id by scanning `council/cards/` for the highest existing `EPIC-` number and
incrementing; `epic: null` on the epic card itself.

**Authorship change:** the epic card's `goal` names what the whole feature
delivers, not any one child's slice of it — but the facilitator does **not**
draft it. The epic goal is authored in wave 1 of step 2 by `principal` as a
**one-line transcription of the human's intake** feature description
(`$ARGUMENTS`). The human is the author of what the product is for;
principal transcribes it into the goal field.

### Step 2 — Decompose into child cards (rewritten)

The decomposition is deliberated by the four SEATS-1 seats in three waves.
The facilitator routes, waits, aggregates verbatim, and authors nothing.

**Wave 1 — `principal` authors the first decomposition artifact.**

Dispatch `principal` once with: the feature (`$ARGUMENTS`), the template,
the board, the assigned epic id, the procedural bars (goal falsifiability,
no colon-space sequence, state rules, Intent-surface rule, em-dash/board
rules), and the mandated output shape. Principal's output, in its native
`Reframe` format:

- the **child decomposition** — the slicing, with per-child `goal`, `state`
  (proposed `Backlog` or `Ready`), and surface flag;
- the **epic goal** — a one-line transcription of the human's intake.

**Wave 2 — `skeptic` + `designer` attack in parallel.**

Dispatch both with **identical input** (principal's artifact), each in its
native format, with the **completeness charter**. Independence preserved:
no input contains another seat's critique.

- `skeptic` attacks each `goal` for falsifiability / stub-satisfiability /
  colon-space and each `state` against the Ready-vs-Backlog bar, with
  runnable checks against the draft text itself.
- `designer` flags which children are surface-touching and argues the
  `Intent` must name the screen/copy/state, in its native `Design position`
  format.

**Completeness charter (scoped per-seat — Skeptic O2 answer).** Attacking
seats attack what's missing as well as what's there; a wholesale rejection
of the slicing is a named disagreement, not a patch request. The charter is
scoped so it never collides with a seat's own body:

- `skeptic` attacks completeness **only in falsifiable form** — e.g. a goal
  satisfiable by a stub, a child whose `state` cannot be deliberated. It
  never files an observational "missing child" objection, because its
  `<how_an_objection_counts>` requires a runnable settling test and a
  missing child has none.
- `principal` and `designer` carry the **observational missing-child
  arguments** in their native formats (principal: seam-cut observations;
  designer: what the person needs).

**Wave 3 — `product-owner` rules, last, unconditionally.**

Dispatch `product-owner` with the amended draft + the disagreement ledger.
Ruling-only:

- ratify or amend the **epic goal** and each child's **`state`**;
- rule each open-judgment dispute the attackers surfaced, **dissent named**,
  in its `Ruling` / `Options rejected` / `Grounding` / `Reversibility`
  format;
- escalate what its `<escalation>` forbids (portfolio change, reversing a
  recorded human decision, the goal itself is the defect) to the human per
  SEATS-1.

It never re-slices children and never rewrites undisputed child goals — that
boundary is what keeps it ruling, not generating.

**Aggregation.** The facilitator aggregates verbatim, labeled by seat, by
mechanical concordance — children aligned by stated scope, agreeing elements
drafted from the agreement and attributed to the seats whose text produced
them, single-source elements attributed to their proposer, and every
conflicting position recorded as a **named disagreement** with both sides
and their job ids. The facilitator never paraphrases a seat's line and never
resolves a disagreement. The facilitator authors nothing at any step.

**Attribution and the disagreement ledger** live at the step-3 gate
presentation and the `runs/` transcript, **never in card files**. The
procedure text must state this explicitly so the facilitator cannot blur the
two.

**Dispatch discipline (restated inline, per the house convention).**
`council_dispatch` → note the returned job id → `council_wait` with a
window → on stall, cancel + one re-dispatch with the same input → on
double-fail, stop and surface to the human. Job ids are on record.

**Participating seats named explicitly (SEATS-1):** product-owner,
designer, principal, skeptic.

### Step 3 — Draft-then-confirm (byte-identical, unchanged)

The step-3 block is byte-identical to today's text (583 bytes, pinned by
test). The **gate presentation** has two clearly-separated parts, and the
separation is defined in step 2's aggregation language (step 3's text does
not change):

1. **Card text exactly as it will be written** — the full draft of the epic
   and every child: complete frontmatter and `Intent` section, exactly as
   each would be written to disk. This is what "exactly as each would be
   written to disk" refers to, and it is byte-faithful to the on-disk card.
2. **A clearly-separate, never-written ledger surface** — per-card
   `Contributors:` line naming every seat whose dispatch produced a
   substantive contribution; a `Disagreements:` block listing any seat that
   did not endorse the card as drafted, each disagreement a one- or
   two-line note naming the seat and the dimension (scope, testability,
   surface, state-assignment), verbatim or a faithful ≤2-line restatement;
   and a `Decision: unresolved — your call` marker on every line of the
   disagreement block. The ledger is **presented, never written** — it does
   not survive onto the on-disk card.

This is the Skeptic O1 answer: the semantic contract "exactly as each would
be written to disk" holds because the card text presented is exactly what
gets written; the ledger is a separate surface that is never written, and
the procedure text states the separation so the facilitator cannot blur the
two. The human's approval at the gate is the resolution of every
disagreement; persisting a pre-approval dispute snapshot to disk would be
stale metadata about a settled question and would corrupt the downstream
`Intent` surface-reading (`council.md` step 1) and the merge check's
criterion 5.
